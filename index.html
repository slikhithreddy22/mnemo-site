Remembers what you did,
not what you saw.

Mnemo is a memory assistant for a chest-worn camera. A small model watches the wearer's hands and decides when something worth remembering happens. Only then does a vision-language model look, and it writes one short, time-stamped line of text. No frame is ever stored. Questions are answered from that diary alone, with times. If the diary has no answer, Mnemo says so.

01Problem

Did I close the fridge? Did I take my pills? For some people, these are hard questions.

People with short-term memory difficulties, from early cognitive decline to executive-function problems, often cannot tell whether they switched off the stove, where they put their keys, or whether they already took today's medicine. The answer was in front of them a few minutes ago. A camera worn on the chest sees those moments from the wearer's own point of view, so walls and furniture don't block it the way they block a fixed room camera.

The hard part is what to do with the video. Our problem statement: build a wearable assistant that turns first-person video into a compact, trustworthy record of the wearer's own actions. It must answer questions about the past, with times, without storing footage and without inventing events.

01 · Storage

Video is costly and intrusive

Hours of footage a day are expensive to keep, slow to search, and capture everyone else in the home.

02 · Compute

Large models can't watch everything

On a T4 GPU, describing every 4-second window took 1.64 s per second of video, which is slower than real time.

03 · Trust

A memory aid must not make things up

For someone who relies on it, a confident wrong answer is worse than no answer. Every answer must point to a recorded moment.

02Objectives

Six objectives, each with a way to check it.

  1. O1

    Decide when to look

    A small always-on model picks the moments with hand–object interaction, so the expensive model runs less often.

    Measured byActions kept vs. evenly spaced calls with the same budget, on people never seen in training.
  2. O2

    Keep a compact, searchable record

    Structured text lines (time, action, object, place) in one SQLite table, instead of captions or video.

    Measured byDiary recall and precision against EPIC-KITCHENS annotations; size on disk.
  3. O3

    Remember the state of things

    Track fridge, oven, tap, stove and other objects as open/closed and on/off, and warn about what was left open.

    Measured byOpening, closing and switching actions found, with and without state memory.
  4. O4

    Answer only from the record

    Give times and places, or reply "I didn't see that in my records." Never guess.

    Measured byAccuracy and invented events, against asking the VLM directly, over one minute and over a whole morning.
  5. O5

    Private by construction

    Frames stay in RAM only while their window is processed. Nothing visual reaches the disk.

    Measured byAn automated test runs the real pipeline with every image-writing function blocked.
  6. O6

    Run on modest hardware

    The small lane runs on a CPU. The large model fits on one 16 GB T4 GPU.

    Measured bySeconds of compute per second of video for each lane.
03Methodology

Two models: a cheap one that is always on, and an expensive one that only looks when asked.

Video is cut into 4-second windows, matching EPIC-KITCHENS-100's own action lengths: the median action lasts 1.6 s, and 81% are shorter than 4 s. The upper lane runs on every frame. The lower lane runs only for windows the small model selects, plus one every 20 seconds as a heartbeat, to keep places and object states current.

  1. 1

    Perception. Each frame becomes a 105-number feature: 45 per hand (landmarks, position, velocity), 13 for the object nearest a hand, and 2 for camera motion. Velocities are measured relative to the background, so walking or turning doesn't look like hand movement.

  2. 2

    Decide when to look. HOI-TCN gives each frame a probability of pick-up, put-down or none. A window is sent on when its score, the maximum of 1 − P(none), reaches τ = 0.6, or when 20 s have passed without a look.

  3. 3

    Describe in fixed lines, not captions. The VLM fills a fixed form with everyday verbs. That cuts output from about 101 to 46 tokens per window, and generation stops at the first repeated line.

    PLACE: kitchen counter
    STATE: fridge = open
    DID: put | milk | fridge
  4. 4

    Remember states. A change from open to closed becomes the row closes fridge. The VLM rarely volunteers "closed" (1 time in 120 windows), so Mnemo asks it again about up to three objects it believes are still open.

  5. 5

    Answer from the diary. The question's time range and keywords select up to 60 rows. The same model answers in one to three sentences using only those rows, with times. Otherwise it replies exactly: "I didn't see that in my records."

  6. 6

    Store nothing visual. A 2.4-hour morning became 636 rows in a 240 kB file. The video is gone as soon as its window is processed.

Inside the small model

Input105 features per frame at 10 fps: hands, nearest object, camera motion
EncodersOne per modality (64 units for hands, 32 for the object, 8 for camera motion), then a 1×1 projection to 96 channels
Temporal blocks4 residual blocks, each with two causal convolutions (kernel 3, dilation 1, 2, 4, 8), layer norm, GELU, dropout 0.2
Receptive field61 frames, about 6 seconds, past frames only, so it runs live
Parameters240,252
Training dataEPIC-KITCHENS-100 take/put, split by person: 22 people for training, 5 for validation, 5 for testing
TrainingAdamW (lr 10⁻³), class-weighted cross-entropy plus a holding head, random modality dropout, early stopping on validation F1
04Results

Measured on people the model never saw.

The test set is 20 random one-minute EPIC-KITCHENS-100 clips from five people held out of training, with 307 annotated actions, 69 of them opening, closing or switching. Qwen3-VL-4B ran on a single Kaggle T4. Scoring is strict: a diary row counts only with the exact EPIC verb and noun class within ±2 s. All systems are scored on the same VLM outputs, so the comparisons are fair even where the absolute numbers look low.

Where it doesn't win

On one-minute clips, asking the VLM directly scores 88% against Mnemo's 76%. Thirty-two frames cover a minute well. The diary pays off over hours.

Tight budgets

Below about 60% of calls, evenly spaced calls find as many actions or slightly more, within noise. τ = 0.6 was read off this curve, so every threshold is reported.

What the numbers cover

Kitchens only, filmed with head-mounted cameras. The morning joins separate clips and has 38 questions. There has been no user study yet.

    06Progress

    From a frame-by-frame prototype to a measured system.

    1. Jul 27, 2026

      Project started

      Repository and project structure. A video source with timestamps and a first motion detector followed in early August.

    2. Aug 2026

      First prototype

      MediaPipe hands, YOLO objects, optical flow, a CLIP zero-shot action classifier and an event binder. Module 1–3 demos on Sep 2.

    3. Oct 15

      Rebuild around HOI-TCN

      One frame cannot tell a pick-up from a put-down, so the prototype was replaced by a causal temporal model on hand, object and camera-motion features, trained on public data split by person.

    4. Oct 16

      Diary and grounded answers

      Qwen3-VL-4B writes the diary, and answers come only from it. Benchmarks on Kaggle T4s: interaction trigger vs. fixed rate, object-state memory, whole-morning questions.

    5. Oct 19

      Ego4D experiment built

      Natural Language Queries such as "Where did I put the drill?" on Ego4D, with development clips kept out of the reported test.

    6. Oct 22

      Paper draft: ENGRAM

      IEEE conference draft: Egocentric Neural Grounded Retrieval of Actions and Moments for Wearable Memory Assistance.

    7. Oct 23

      Open source

      Code, trained trigger and documentation published under the MIT licence.

    8. Next

      Ego4D full run in progress

      Episodic-memory questions beyond kitchens, on clips held out from all tuning.

    9. Planned

      Search, real recordings, app planned

      Hybrid keyword and vector search over the diary, continuous chest-camera recordings, a mobile app, and a user study with ethical approval.
